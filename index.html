Great White Shark

A few words about Great White Shark

Great white sharks are large predatory sharks that live in coastal waters around the world.
They are easily recognised by their grey upper body, white underside, powerful tail and rows of sharp teeth.

Great white sharks are excellent hunters. They mainly eat fish, seals and other marine animals.
They have an extremely strong sense of smell and can also detect tiny electrical signals produced by other animals in the water.

Despite their reputation, great white sharks do not normally hunt humans for food.
They are important predators in marine ecosystems and help maintain a natural balance between different ocean species.

A few facts about Great White Shark

  • Great white sharks can grow to around 6 metres long.
  • They can detect the electrical signals produced by other animals.
  • Great whites can swim at speeds of around 25 mph (40 km/h) in short bursts.
  • Great white sharks can sometimes jump completely out of the water while hunting seals.
  • They can have around 300 teeth arranged in several rows.
